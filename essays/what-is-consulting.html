What Is Consulting, Really?
(And Why Does Everyone Suddenly Want to Do It?)

Walk into any career counseling session or LinkedIn scroll lately, and chances are you’ll hear someone say they’re “interested in consulting.” It’s become one of those buzzwords that gets tossed around so casually that it starts to lose meaning — like “strategy” or “innovation.”

I know this because I was one of those people. A few months ago, if you’d asked me what management consultants actually do, I might’ve said something vague like “solve business problems” or “help companies grow.” Not wrong — but not helpful either.

So, I decided to dig deeper.

Consulting, Demystified (Kind Of)

At its core, consulting is about helping organizations make better decisions. That’s it. Consultants are like temporary teammates brought in to solve a tricky, high-stakes problem that the client either:

  • Doesn’t have time to solve,
  • Doesn’t have the skills or data to solve, or
  • Needs an outsider’s point of view on.

These problems could be as complex as “Should we enter the African telecom market next year?” or as operational as “How can we reduce our logistics costs by 20%?”

What makes it consulting and not just business work is that you’re coming in from the outside, with limited context, and expected to get up to speed fast, break down the problem, do structured analysis, and deliver clear recommendations — all while managing client expectations and maybe teaching them a few things along the way.

Why the Hype?

There are a few reasons why students (especially in business and STEM fields) flock to consulting:

  • Prestige & exposure: You get to work with big-name companies on high-impact projects — sometimes with C-level executives from day one.
  • Learning curve: It’s one of the few jobs where you're paid to learn — new industries, markets, business models — project after project.
  • Exit opportunities: Consulting is seen as a launchpad to almost anything: entrepreneurship, finance, product management, even policy work.

But I also think consulting attracts people who enjoy a certain type of mental workout — structured problem-solving, fast learning, and high-pressure thinking. If that sounds like fun (it does to me), then it makes sense.

My First Look at the Consulting World

I came to consulting through a somewhat odd path: I was deeply interested in trading and financial markets — chart patterns, macro news, risk management, all of it. But the more I studied markets, the more I found myself fascinated by how businesses actually make decisions. I wanted to zoom out — from price movements to strategy.

So I started reading books like Case in Point and Case Interview Secrets, and I enrolled in a consulting course from Emory University that breaks down the consulting process: hypothesis-driven thinking, data collection, industry benchmarking, client communication, the works. It was like opening a toolbox I didn’t know I needed.

What surprised me the most? How structured everything is. Consultants don’t just “figure things out.” They follow a disciplined approach to breaking down problems (MECE structures), forming hypotheses, testing those hypotheses with data, and then presenting insights in a logical, persuasive way — often with very little time and even less data than you’d like.

Still Learning, Still Curious

To be clear: I’m not a consultant (yet). I’m a second-year undergraduate trying to understand what makes this industry tick. But I’m convinced that consulting is about much more than suits, slides, or late-night flights. It’s about clear thinking, curiosity, and learning how to make better decisions — under pressure, with stakes, and for real impact.

If you’re also curious about consulting but not sure what it really involves, here’s my advice: don’t worry about the buzzwords. Start with the problems. What kinds of problems do you want to solve? What kinds of people do you want to solve them with?

The rest will follow.
