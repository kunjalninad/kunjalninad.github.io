A Guide To Value Investing

Published on Mar 09, 2026

This is a reading curriculum aimed at developing a Graham-Buffett style value investor. It is designed to be cognitively gentle at the start, then progressively more technical. The goal is to "absorb" the books rather than memorize all the points.

If the books feel slow or repetitive, that is the point. Value investing is not very stimulating.
Great books often repeat the same point from different angles multiple times.

Also, this list is by no means exhaustive. You're better off treating it as a philosophical introduction to value investing, rather than a professional-level investing syllabus.

Part 01

These shape how great investors think, before touching accounting.

  1. The Psychology of Money — Morgan Housel
  2. Poor Charlie’s Almanack — Charles T. Munger
  3. The Little Book of Value Investing — Christopher Browne
  4. The Essays of Warren Buffett — Lawrence Cunningham

Part 02

Foundational, still accessible.

  1. The Intelligent Investor — Benjamin Graham

Part 03

Accounting & Business Fundamentals. Take your time with this one. Go slowly.

  1. The Accounting Game: Basic Accounting Fresh From The Lemonade Stand — Orloff & Mullis
  2. Financial Statements — Thomas Ittelson
  3. Financial Statement Analysis — Martin Fridson
  4. The Five Rules for Successful Stock Investing — Pat Dorsey
  5. Warren Buffett and the Interpretation of Financial Statements — Mary Buffett
  6. Quality of Earnings — Thornton O’Glove

Part 04

  1. The Outsiders — William Thorndike
  2. Competition Demystified — Bruce Greenwald
  3. Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits — Philip Fisher
  4. Value Investing: From Graham to Buffett and Beyond — Bruce Greenwald
  5. Berkshire Hathaway Annual Letters

You don't have to read all of these. As always, listen to your gut.
If a book just doesn't "feel" right for you, drop it.
Happy investing!
